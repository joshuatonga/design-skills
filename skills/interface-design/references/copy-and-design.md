# Product copy and design review

Apply while writing interfaces and again before completion. Fix findings within the requested scope directly.

## Copy that earns its place

Explain the user's task, useful result, or meaningful product distinction. Use short, specific language with the product's tone. Personality is welcome when it communicates something; do not replace clear positioning with motivational filler.

| Surface | Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product description | Your documents, reimagined | Fill out and sign PDFs on your Mac. |
| Onboarding | Let's make some magic happen | Create your first project. |
| Empty state | Your canvas awaits | No documents yet. Create one to get started. |
| Action | Make it happen | Create project |
| Success | Your masterpiece is ready | Your PDF is ready to download. |
| Error | Oops! We hit a little bump | Couldn't save your changes. Try again. |

Avoid interchangeable constructions such as “A little space for…”, “Make room for…”, “Less X, more Y”, “Where X meets Y”, “Say hello to…”, “Meet your new…”, “Everything you need, nothing you don't”, “Built for the way you work”, “Focus on what matters”, “Unlock your potential”, “Supercharge your workflow”, “Elevate your experience”, “Beautifully simple”, “Effortlessly powerful”, “It's time to rethink…”, and “The future of X is here”. Do not substitute another vague slogan. Use words such as seamless, effortless, delightful, intuitive, powerful, beautiful, and revolutionary only when they contribute concrete meaning.

Name actions with verbs and objects where useful. Use one label for the same intent across a flow, while distinguishing different outcomes such as saving a draft and publishing. Confirm destructive actions with the affected item and consequence. Errors should identify the failed action and useful recovery, preserve entered data, and avoid blaming the user. Announce success only after the relevant outcome is confirmed.

## Design around the workflow

Choose structure from the content: lists for repeated records, tables for comparisons, forms for input, and cards when bounded grouping helps. Do not place every section in a rounded card or default every marketing page to three equal feature columns. Keep primary actions discoverable and density appropriate to the job.

Use a coherent type, spacing, shape, and semantic color system. Keep the established brand, including justified multiple accents or type pairings. With no existing brand, choose a restrained direction and state the roles of its tokens before spreading them across screens.

Remove oversized vague heroes, gratuitous gradients/glows, glass on every card, floating decoration, meaningless illustrations, and whitespace that makes useful content harder to reach. System materials on native controls are legitimate platform behavior; do not remove them under a blanket anti-glass rule. Emoji can belong to user content or a deliberate brand voice, but should not substitute for recognizable interface controls.

## Final pass

- Does each heading help identify the task, content, or product distinction?
- Do buttons describe their actual effects, and do repeated intents use consistent labels?
- Are empty, loading, error, and success states specific, useful, and truthful?
- Can motivational copy, duplicate explanations, or decorative elements be removed without losing meaning?
- Is hierarchy clear with representative content, long labels, and relevant accessibility settings?
- Do layout, controls, and visual treatment fit this product and platform?
- Have observed findings been corrected and affected behavior rechecked?

Keep a final report focused on material decisions and remaining limits; a long copy-review transcript is unnecessary.
